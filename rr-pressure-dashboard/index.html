How we defined pressure

Pressure is how much one delivery can still change the result, not a single rule of thumb.

Win-probability model

Two gradient-boosted models, one per innings, estimate the batting side's chance from runs, wickets, balls left, run rate and, in chases, runs needed and required rate. Cross-validated by match: log-loss 0.638 and 0.408 against 0.693 for a coin flip.

Leverage Index

For each legal ball we simulate the seven outcomes, re-score win probability and take the weighted absolute swing. The average ball is 1.0; LI ≥ 1.5 is high pressure.

Why it works

Dot-ball rate climbs from 33.5% to 38.6% as leverage rises, and pressure balls sit in closer games: 0.16 from 50/50 against 0.24 on normal balls.

Method and fair comparison

Pressure Impact · 80%

Win Probability Added summed over pressure balls, per 100 balls, shrunk toward zero so a hot streak cannot beat a long record.

Consistency · 20%

Share of pressure innings or spells (4+ balls faced, 6+ delivered) with positive WPA, smoothed toward 50%.

Eligibility

Batters: 90+ pressure balls and 15 innings (78 qualify). Bowlers: 72+ balls and 15 spells (58 qualify). Associate, A-team, rain-hit and no-result games removed.

Guardrail

Clutch Lift (pressure minus normal) was noise in split-half tests, so it only removes players more than 1.5 SD below the pool.

Reliability and limitations

Bootstrap stability

Across 300 match re-samples, Narine (89%), David (84%) and Kuldeep (80%) are most secure. Membership is robust; exact order is not.

Split-half reliability

Pressure Impact correlates 0.21 (batters) and 0.29 (bowlers) between halves, about 0.34 and 0.45 after Spearman-Brown.

Sensitivity

LI 1.2 or 60/40 weights keep 9 of 10 names. LI 2.0 keeps only 5 batters and 3 bowlers, so extreme-leverage ranks are not trusted.

Not modelled

Ball-level opposition quality, pitch and dew, fielding, batting position and venue. Confirm 2027 auction eligibility.
